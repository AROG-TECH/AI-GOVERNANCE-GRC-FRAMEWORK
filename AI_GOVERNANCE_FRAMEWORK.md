# Mini AI Governance and Risk Framework for Nonprofits

## Overview

This framework provides nonprofits with essential governance and risk management structures for responsible AI adoption and deployment. It balances innovation with ethical considerations, stakeholder protection, and organizational sustainability.

## Executive Summary

### Purpose
This Mini AI Governance and Risk Framework enables nonprofits to:
- Safely and ethically integrate AI tools and systems
- Mitigate risks related to bias, privacy, and misuse
- Maintain stakeholder trust and organizational accountability
- Align AI initiatives with nonprofit mission and values
- Ensure compliance with relevant regulations

### Scope
- **Organization Type**: Nonprofit organizations (all sectors)
- **Application**: AI systems, tools, and automation initiatives
- **Scale**: Adaptable for small to mid-size nonprofits
- **Timeline**: Continuous governance and risk monitoring

## AI Governance Pillars

### 1. Strategy & Leadership

**Objectives:**
- Establish clear AI governance roles and responsibilities
- Define organizational AI strategy aligned with mission
- Ensure leadership commitment and oversight

**Key Components:**

| Component | Description | Responsibility |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| **AI Governance Committee** | Oversees AI initiatives, policies, and risk management | Executive Director, Board Representative, Tech Lead |
| **AI Strategy** | Defines how AI supports nonprofit mission and goals | Strategy Team with stakeholder input |
| **Roles & Responsibilities** | Clear designations for AI oversight and implementation | HR/Leadership |
| **Board Oversight** | Regular reporting to Board on AI initiatives and risks | Executive Director/Committee Chair |

**Actions:**
- [ ] Establish AI Governance Committee with defined charter
- [ ] Document AI strategy aligned with nonprofit mission
- [ ] Define roles (AI Lead, Ethics Lead, Compliance Lead)
- [ ] Schedule quarterly board updates on AI initiatives

---

### 2. Ethics & Values Alignment

**Objectives:**
- Embed ethical principles in AI decision-making
- Ensure AI initiatives align with nonprofit values
- Prevent harm to beneficiaries and stakeholders

**Key Principles:**

| Principle | Definition | Application |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Transparency** | Clear disclosure of AI use and limitations | Inform beneficiaries when AI is used in decisions |
| **Fairness** | Prevent bias and discrimination | Audit AI systems for disparate impact |
| **Accountability** | Clear responsibility for AI outcomes | Designate owners for each AI system |
| **Privacy** | Protect personal data and sensitive information | Implement data minimization and security |
| **Beneficence** | AI should benefit intended populations | Assess positive impact on mission |

**Actions:**
- [ ] Adopt AI ethics principles statement
- [ ] Conduct ethics review for new AI projects
- [ ] Establish bias testing procedures
- [ ] Create feedback mechanism for stakeholder concerns

## Risk Management Framework

### Risk Categories for Nonprofits

#### 1. **Bias & Discrimination Risk**

**Description:** AI systems perpetuating or amplifying historical biases

**Examples:**
- Fundraising algorithms targeting certain demographics unfairly
- Program eligibility AI discriminating against protected classes
- Hiring systems biased against certain groups

**Mitigation Strategies:**
- Regular bias audits using representative data
- Diverse training data reflecting served populations
- Human review of high-impact decisions
- Feedback mechanism for potential bias complaints
- Documentation of model development and validation

**Metrics:**
- Disparate impact analysis by demographic group
- False positive/negative rates by population
- Audit frequency and findings

---

#### 2. **Privacy & Data Security Risk**

**Description:** Unauthorized access, misuse, or breach of personal data

**Examples:**
- Beneficiary data exposed in AI system breach
- AI processing personal information without consent
- Retention of data beyond intended purpose

**Mitigation Strategies:**
- Privacy impact assessment before AI deployment
- Data minimization (collect only necessary data)
- Encryption of sensitive data
- Access controls and authentication
- Regular security audits and penetration testing
- Incident response plan
- Compliance with GDPR, CCPA, and local regulations

**Metrics:**
- Data security incidents and resolution time
- Compliance audit results
- Staff privacy training completion rates

---

#### 3. **Accuracy & Reliability Risk**

**Description:** AI systems making incorrect predictions or decisions

**Examples:**
- Needs assessment AI incorrectly identifying vulnerable populations
- Predictive models with high error rates
- AI system failures causing service disruptions

**Mitigation Strategies:**
- Validation of model accuracy before deployment
- Testing in limited pilots before full rollout
- Monitoring model performance over time
- Threshold for human review (decisions below confidence threshold)
- Fallback to human decision-making if accuracy degrades
- Regular retraining as new data becomes available

**Metrics:**
- Model accuracy metrics (precision, recall, F1-score)
- False positive and false negative rates
- System uptime and availability
- Error reporting and resolution

---

#### 4. **Transparency & Explainability Risk**

**Description:** Stakeholders unable to understand how AI made decisions

**Examples:**
- "Black box" AI systems making decisions without explanation
- Beneficiaries unaware AI influenced decisions affecting them
- Staff unable to override AI recommendations appropriately

**Mitigation Strategies:**
- Use explainable AI models where possible
- Document decision logic and model assumptions
- Provide clear explanations to affected individuals
- Training staff on AI limitations and appropriate skepticism
- Appeal and override mechanisms for stakeholders
- Regular user testing and feedback on explanations

**Metrics:**
- Staff comprehension of AI decision-making (training assessments)
- Stakeholder satisfaction with explanations
- Appeal/override frequency and outcomes

---

#### 5. **Accountability & Governance Risk**

**Description:** Unclear responsibility for AI system outcomes

**Examples:**
- AI system causes harm but responsibility is unclear
- No oversight of AI decisions or performance
- Lack of policies or procedures governing AI use

**Mitigation Strategies:**
- Clear designation of AI system owners
- Regular governance committee meetings
- Documentation of all AI systems and their purposes
- Policies covering development, deployment, monitoring, and decommissioning
- Incident response procedures
- Third-party audit of high-risk systems

**Metrics:**
- Governance committee meeting frequency and attendance
- Policy documentation and staff awareness (training completion)
- System inventory completeness
- Incident response time

---

#### 6. **Operational & Integration Risk**

**Description:** Technical failures, integration issues, or operational challenges

**Examples:**
- AI system integration fails with existing systems
- Staff not trained adequately leading to misuse
- AI system failures causing service disruptions

**Mitigation Strategies:**
- Technical design review before implementation
- User acceptance testing with staff and stakeholders
- Comprehensive training and documentation
- Change management process
- Monitoring and alerting for system health
- Disaster recovery and business continuity plans
- Gradual rollout with fallback options

**Metrics:**
- Deployment success rate
- Staff proficiency and satisfaction (surveys/assessments)
- System performance and uptime
- Training completion rates

---

#### 7. **Reputational & Trust Risk**

**Description:** Negative perception or loss of trust due to AI misuse

**Examples:**
- Media coverage of AI system making harmful decisions
- Donor concerns about ethical AI use
- Beneficiaries losing confidence in organization

**Mitigation Strategies:**
- Stakeholder engagement and communication
- Ethics review before major initiatives
- Transparent disclosure of AI limitations
- Proactive messaging about AI governance
- Crisis communication plan
- Regular stakeholder feedback and engagement
- Alignment with industry best practices

**Metrics:**
- Stakeholder trust surveys
- Media mentions and sentiment analysis
- Social media engagement and feedback
- Donor retention and satisfaction

### Risk Assessment Matrix

**Risk Level Determination:**

```
           Likelihood (L)
         Low | Med | High
Impact (I)  
  Low     | G  | Y  | Y  |
  Medium  | Y  | Y  | R  |
  High    | Y  | R  | R  |

G = Green (Low Risk)
Y = Yellow (Medium Risk)  
R = Red (High Risk)

Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact
```

**Risk Response Strategy:**

| Risk Level | Response | Action |
|-----------|----------|--------|
| **Green** | Accept | Monitor; no specific controls needed |
| **Yellow** | Mitigate | Implement controls; monitor regularly |
| **Red** | Reduce/Avoid | Implement strong controls or avoid AI use |

---

### Risk Assessment Process

**Step 1: Risk Identification**
- Review all AI systems and initiatives
- Conduct scenario analysis
- Gather input from staff and stakeholders

**Step 2: Risk Analysis**
- Assess likelihood (1-5 scale)
- Assess potential impact (1-5 scale)
- Calculate risk score (L × I)
- Determine risk level (Green/Yellow/Red)

**Step 3: Risk Mitigation Planning**
- For Yellow/Red risks: Develop mitigation strategies
- Assign ownership and accountability
- Set implementation timeline

**Step 4: Implementation**
- Execute mitigation strategies
- Monitor control effectiveness
- Document results

**Step 5: Monitoring & Review**
- Quarterly risk reviews
- Annual comprehensive assessment
- Ongoing monitoring of key metrics

## Monitoring and Compliance

### Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

#### Governance KPIs
- [ ] Governance committee meeting frequency (Target: Quarterly minimum)
- [ ] Board oversight report completion (Target: Quarterly)
- [ ] Policy documentation and review (Target: Annual)
- [ ] AI system inventory accuracy (Target: 100%)

#### Risk Management KPIs
- [ ] Risk assessments completed (Target: 100% of systems)
- [ ] Mitigation plans for Yellow/Red risks (Target: 100%)
- [ ] Mitigation control implementation rate (Target: 90%+)
- [ ] Risk review frequency (Target: Quarterly)

#### Compliance KPIs
- [ ] Audit findings resolution rate (Target: 100%)
- [ ] Data security incidents (Target: Zero unauthorized breaches)
- [ ] Staff training completion (Target: 100%)
- [ ] Policy acknowledgment rate (Target: 100% of staff)

#### Stakeholder KPIs
- [ ] Stakeholder trust survey results (Target: Maintain/improve satisfaction)
- [ ] Transparency disclosure completion (Target: 100%)
- [ ] Complaint/concern resolution time (Target: <30 days)
- [ ] Feedback mechanism engagement (Target: Regular usage)

---

### Monitoring Mechanisms

**1. Quarterly Risk Review**
- Review existing risk assessments
- Identify new risks
- Update mitigation status
- Report to governance committee

**2. Annual Comprehensive Audit**
- Third-party audit of governance framework
- Review of all AI systems and compliance
- Assessment of policy effectiveness
- Recommendations for improvement

**3. Continuous Monitoring**
- Dashboard tracking KPIs
- Automated alerts for security incidents
- Regular system performance checks
- Stakeholder feedback channels

**4. Incident Management**
- Documented incident response procedures
- Root cause analysis for serious issues
- Corrective action implementation
- Post-incident review and learning
